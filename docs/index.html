The PHIA probability yardstick

The UK Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment (PHIA) yardstick links words to probability ranges so 'likely' means the same thing to everyone. Roughly: remote chance (under 5%), highly unlikely (10-20%), unlikely (25-35%), realistic possibility (40-50%), likely (55-75%), highly likely (80-90%), almost certain (95% and above).

Think of it like this: Like a weather forecast saying '70% chance of rain' instead of 'it might rain'. Everyone knows how seriously to take it.
Why it matters for UK clients

The NCSC and UK government assessments use this language. Using it in client reports makes your intel sound professional and makes it hard to misread.

Enrichment tip

After enriching a story, write one assessment sentence with a yardstick term, for example: 'It is likely (55-75%) that UK retailers will be targeted with this technique this quarter.'

Traffic Light Protocol (TLP 2.0)

TLP labels tell the reader who they may share information with. TLP:CLEAR = anyone. TLP:GREEN = your community, not public. TLP:AMBER = your organisation and its clients who need to know. TLP:AMBER+STRICT = your organisation only. TLP:RED = named recipients only, no further sharing.

NCSC Early Warning: free enrichment for UK clients

Early Warning is a free NCSC service. An organisation registers its IP addresses and domain names, and the NCSC sends alerts when those assets show up in threat feeds: infections, open vulnerable services, or links to attacker infrastructure.

What is data enrichment?

Enrichment means taking a bare fact (an IP address, a domain, a CVE number) and adding context until someone can make a decision with it. A raw IP tells you nothing. An IP that belongs to a bulletproof hosting company, was seen yesterday running a known ransomware C2 server, and talked to your client's firewall at 3am tells you to act.

Show answerTo add enough context to a raw indicator that someone can make a decision with it.
