Okta vulnerabilities

Answered · re-checked today
Bottom line: No Okta or Auth0 vulnerability is known to be exploited. The real risk to Okta customers is social engineering: phone-call phishing (vishing) that tricks staff or the help desk into handing over sign-ins and MFA.
0on CISA KEV today: None on the exploited list

0new CVEs not yet in the answer
Patch batch: September 2026 advisories Medium concern
Okta published fixes for bugs in its self-hosted components, which Okta found by scanning its own code with AI. Okta reports no evidence of exploitation, and none are on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list (catalogue 2026.10.04). Okta's cloud service is patched by Okta; customers only need to act if they run the components below.
Active threat: vishing against Okta SSO High concern
Since January 2026, groups linked to ShinyHunters, Scattered Spider and Lapsus$ ('Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters') have called staff and IT help desks, steering victims through fake Okta login pages in real time to capture passwords and MFA approvals. Push MFA with number matching does not stop it. Once inside Okta, they move into connected SaaS apps (Salesforce, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace) and steal data for extortion. Reported targets include financial services.
Assessment

It is unlikely (25-35%) that the September Okta bugs are being exploited at scale today, but a realistic possibility (40-50%) that exploitation follows now that details are public. It is highly likely (80-90%) that vishing against Okta-using organisations continues through the rest of 2026.

Moderate confidence. KEV and the CVE records were checked directly; Okta's own advisory pages could not be reached and are cited from search results.

Recommended actions
  1. Check whether you run any of the listed self-hosted components and upgrade them to the fixed versions.
  2. If you used the Hyperdrive installer, rotate the OAuth client secret and clear old installer and agent logs, since they may hold secrets or SAML assertions.
  3. Move admins and help desk staff to phishing-resistant MFA (Okta FastPass, FIDO2 security keys or passkeys), then everyone else.
  4. Use Okta network zones to allow sign-ins only from expected networks and block anonymisers (VPNs, Tor, proxies).
  5. Tighten help desk checks before any password or MFA reset, for example a call back to a known number or manager approval.
  6. Watch the Okta System Log for new MFA enrolments or resets, sign-ins from new devices or locations, and new admin role grants, and alert on them.

Lesson 279 · Core models · cycle of 33, repeats on purpose

IOC vs IOA vs TTP

An IOC (indicator of compromise) is evidence something bad happened: a hash, IP or domain. An IOA (indicator of attack) is behaviour showing an attack in progress, like PowerShell downloading a file and disabling antivirus. TTPs are the attacker's habits described in general terms.

Think of it like this: IOC = fingerprints left at the scene. IOA = someone seen trying door handles right now. TTP = 'this gang always comes in through the back garden'.
Why it matters for UK clients

Most free feeds give IOCs. Clients with a SIEM or EDR get more value if you translate IOCs into IOAs and detection ideas.

Enrichment tip

For every IOC batch, add one line: 'what behaviour would we see if this hit us?' That turns IOCs into IOAs.

Passive DNS and certificate transparency

Passive DNS is a history of which domains pointed to which IPs over time. Certificate transparency (crt.sh) logs every public TLS certificate issued. Together they let you pivot: from one bad domain to the IP, to other domains on that IP, to certificates with similar names.

Quick quiz: What is 'pivoting' in threat intel?
Show answerUsing one known indicator to discover related indicators, for example from a domain to its IP to other domains on that IP.
Review · first seen 3 days ago

IPs, ASNs and RIPE

Every public IP belongs to a network called an Autonomous System, identified by an ASN (for example AS2856 is BT). RIPE NCC is the registry for Europe, including the UK. RIPEstat (stat.ripe.net) shows who owns an IP, its ASN, country and routing history for free.

Quick quiz: Which regional registry covers UK IP addresses?
Show answerRIPE NCC.
Review · first seen 7 days ago

The PHIA probability yardstick

The UK Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment (PHIA) yardstick links words to probability ranges so 'likely' means the same thing to everyone. Roughly: remote chance (under 5%), highly unlikely (10-20%), unlikely (25-35%), realistic possibility (40-50%), likely (55-75%), highly likely (80-90%), almost certain (95% and above).

Show answerLikely (or probable).
