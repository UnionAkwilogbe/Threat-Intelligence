Lesson 273 · Core models · cycle of 33, repeats on purpose

The intelligence cycle

Intel work runs in a loop: Direction (what do we need to know?), Collection (gather data), Processing (clean and structure it), Analysis (make sense of it), Dissemination (share it), Feedback (was it useful?). Then round again.

Think of it like this: Like cooking for a client: ask what they want to eat, buy ingredients, prep them, cook, serve, and ask how it tasted.
Why it matters for UK clients

Most weak UK client intel programmes skip Direction and Feedback. They collect everything and ask nothing, so the output is noise.

Enrichment tip

Enrichment lives in Processing and Analysis. If you do not know the client's questions (Direction), you do not know which enrichment matters.

Try it today

Write down one question a client of yours would want answered every morning. That is a requirement.

Quiz: Which two stages of the cycle are most often skipped?
Show answerDirection (setting requirements) and Feedback.

The PHIA probability yardstick

The UK Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment (PHIA) yardstick links words to probability ranges so 'likely' means the same thing to everyone. Roughly: remote chance (under 5%), highly unlikely (10-20%), unlikely (25-35%), realistic possibility (40-50%), likely (55-75%), highly likely (80-90%), almost certain (95% and above).

Quick quiz: Which yardstick term covers roughly 55-75%?
Show answerLikely (or probable).
Review · first seen 3 days ago

The Admiralty Code: grading sources

The Admiralty (NATO) system grades intel with a letter and a number. Letter = source reliability (A completely reliable to E unreliable, F cannot be judged). Number = information credibility (1 confirmed by other sources to 5 improbable, 6 cannot be judged). B2 means usually reliable source, probably true.

Quick quiz: In the grade 'B2', what does the B describe and what does the 2 describe?
Show answerB is source reliability (usually reliable). 2 is information credibility (probably true).
Review · first seen 7 days ago

The Pyramid of Pain

David Bianco's Pyramid of Pain ranks indicators by how much it hurts the attacker when you block them. Bottom to top: hash values (trivial to change), IP addresses (easy), domain names (simple), network and host artefacts (annoying), tools (challenging), TTPs: tactics, techniques and procedures (tough).

Quick quiz: Which is harder for an attacker to change: an IP address or a technique (TTP)?
Show answerA technique. TTPs sit at the top of the pyramid because changing them means changing how the attacker works.
