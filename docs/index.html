Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIRs)

PIRs are the handful of questions a client most needs answered, written down and agreed. Example: 'Is any ransomware group targeting UK law firms using our VPN vendor?' Every feed and enrichment step should serve at least one PIR.

Think of it like this: A shopping list. Without one you come home with 40 things and forget the milk.
Why it matters for UK clients

Tie PIRs to UK specifics: the client's sector, their regulator (FCA, ICO, Ofcom, Ofgem), their key suppliers, and their main technology.

Enrichment tip

Store PIRs per client as keywords (sector, vendors, brands). Then auto-tag every incoming story against them. This dashboard's sector tags are a first step.

Try it today

Write three PIRs for one real or imagined UK client.

The intelligence cycle

Intel work runs in a loop: Direction (what do we need to know?), Collection (gather data), Processing (clean and structure it), Analysis (make sense of it), Dissemination (share it), Feedback (was it useful?). Then round again.

Review · first seen 3 days ago

Traffic Light Protocol (TLP 2.0)

TLP labels tell the reader who they may share information with. TLP:CLEAR = anyone. TLP:GREEN = your community, not public. TLP:AMBER = your organisation and its clients who need to know. TLP:AMBER+STRICT = your organisation only. TLP:RED = named recipients only, no further sharing.

Quick quiz: Which TLP label means 'share with anyone, publicly'?
Show answerTLP:CLEAR.
Review · first seen 7 days ago

CVSS vs EPSS vs KEV

CVSS (0-10) says how bad a bug could be if exploited. EPSS (0-1, from FIRST.org) estimates the chance it will be exploited in the next 30 days. KEV (CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list) says it is already being exploited. Severity, likelihood, reality.

Quick quiz: Which of the three tells you a vulnerability is already being used in real attacks?
Show answerKEV. CVSS is severity and EPSS is predicted likelihood.
